Math Facts Practice

Practise addition, subtraction and division in short rounds. Choose the kind of questions and how big the numbers should be, then see which facts need another try. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere.

Round finished

How to use it

  1. Start small: numbers up to 10 for early learners, up to 20 once those feel easy, and up to 100 for older children.
  2. Pick one type of question at a time until it feels comfortable, then try Mixed.
  3. Type each answer and press Enter or tap Check.
  4. Look at the facts you missed at the end and practise those again tomorrow.

Mental math tricks worth teaching

  • Make 10. To add 8 + 5, take 2 from the 5 to make 8 + 2 = 10, then add the remaining 3 to get 13.
  • Use doubles. If 6 + 6 = 12, then 6 + 7 is just one more, which is 13.
  • Count up for subtraction. To work out 15 − 9, start at 9 and count up to 15: that is 6 steps.
  • Think of division as sharing. 24 ÷ 6 asks "how many sixes fit in 24?" and the times tables give the answer: 4.
  • Check with the opposite operation. If 13 − 5 = 8, then 8 + 5 should give 13 again.

A gentle practice routine

Five minutes a day is plenty. Try one type of question for a few days, and celebrate a personal best (more correct answers or a faster round) rather than comparing with anyone else. If your child is getting frustrated, drop back to smaller numbers: confidence matters more than speed.

Questions

Does it keep track of my score?

No. Results disappear when you close or reload the page, and nothing is sent to a server.

Why does division only use whole numbers?

The questions are built from the times tables, so every division question has a whole-number answer. This keeps the practice focused on number facts.

For times tables, try the times table practice and the printable multiplication chart. For a game version, play Balloon Math Hero.
